About PseudoToolkit

PseudoToolkit is an independent research archive and project dedicated to exposing how junk science, unregulated forensic evaluations, and pseudo-psychological concepts are weaponized within family court systems.

Who are we?

Mission

When high-conflict custody litigation intersects with allegations of domestic violence, coercive control, or child mistreatment, family courts frequently replace rigorous evidentiary standards with subjective diagnostic labels. Our objective is to bridge the gap between mainstream scientific consensus and courtroom practice by:

  • Providing Information:
  • Deconstructing Institutional Biases: Examining how statutory mandates—such as "friendly parent" doctrines—and private custody evaluation pipelines are used to penalize protective parents who raise legitimate safety concerns.
  • Supporting Evidentiary Integrity: Providing litigants, advocates, and researchers with the documentation, case precedents, and statutory analyses needed to challenge non-empirical testimony under state and federal evidentiary rules (Daubert / Frye).

Research Scope & Methodology

All materials, statutory critiques, and case summaries hosted on PseudoToolkit are compiled from public records, peer-reviewed clinical studies, published appellate decisions, legislative hearing transcripts, and reports issued by governmental agencies and established legal institutions.

We do not provide direct legal representation or formal psychological evaluations. Rather, our focus is educational: equipping parents, attorneys, and child welfare advocates with the tools necessary to recognize and counter pseudo-scientific claims before they compromise a child's safety.
