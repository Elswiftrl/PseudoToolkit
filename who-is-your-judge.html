Who Really Is Your Judge?

In family courts, the judge's opinion is the one you have to cater to. Even if something seems like common sense or common knowledge, it may not apply to your judge. Catering to their personality is key to not only providing stronger defense to "Junk Science" claims but also helpful emotionally when things do not go in your way.[cite: 10]

This section highlights who judges are and how they come to power.[cite: 10]

Antisocial PD in the Workplace

What is Antisocial Personality Disorder

Antisocial Personality Disorder is defined by the American Psychiatric Association as follows:[cite: 10]

Diagnostic Criteria Antisocial Personality Disorder F60.2

A. A pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others, occurring since age 15 years, as indicated by three (or more) of the following:[cite: 4, 10]

  1. Failure to conform to social norms with respect to lawful behaviors, as indicated by repeatedly performing acts that are grounds for arrest.[cite: 4, 10]
  2. Deceitfulness, as indicated by repeated lying, use of aliases, or conning others for personal profit or pleasure.[cite: 4, 10]
  3. Impulsivity or failure to plan ahead.[cite: 4, 10]
  4. Irritability and aggressiveness, as indicated by repeated physical fights or assaults.[cite: 4, 10]
  5. Reckless disregard for safety of self or others.[cite: 4, 10]
  6. Consistent irresponsibility, as indicated by repeated failure to sustain consistent work behavior or honor financial obligations.[cite: 4, 10]
  7. Lack of remorse, as indicated by being indifferent to or rationalizing having hurt, mistreated, or stolen from another.[cite: 4, 10]

B. The individual is at least age 18 years.[cite: 4, 10]

C. There is evidence of conduct disorder with onset before age 15 years.[cite: 4, 10]

D. The occurrence of antisocial behavior is not exclusively during the course of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.[cite: 4, 10]

Source: American Psychiatric Association. (2022). Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (5th ed., text rev.) (DSM-5-TR). Reprinted under fair use for educational and comparative legal analysis.[cite: 10]

Research Your Specific Judge

Image source: ballotpedia.org[cite: 10]

This is Keven M. P. O’Grady and he is a judge that has been welcoming to such "Junk Science" claims. He was elected by former Kansas Governor Sam Brownback in 2012.[cite: 10]

In the same year of 2012, the Governor who appointed O'Grady, Brownback, faced one of the worst approval ratings of all governors in the country.[cite: 10]

Sam Brownback Gubernatorial Tenure

Brownback took office in January 2011, in the early years of national recovery from the Great Recession. Also in 2011, Republicans resumed control of the Kansas House of Representatives with their largest majority in half a century. Most Republicans in the Kansas Legislature were members of the Tea Party movement who shared Brownback's conservative views.[cite: 10]

Two of Brownback's major stated goals were to reduce taxes and to increase spending on education.[cite: 10]

By April 2012, Brownback had an approval rating of 34 percent according to a Survey USA Poll. A Republican polling company found his approval rating to be 51 percent in May 2012. In November 2015, Brownback had an approval rating of 26 percent according to a Morning Consult poll, the lowest among all governors in the United States. Three separate polls between November 2015 and September 2016 ranked Brownback as the nation's least-popular governor—a September 2016 poll showing an approval rating of 23%. In the state elections of 2016—seen largely as a referendum on Brownback's policies and administration—Brownback's supporters in the legislature suffered major defeats. In 2017 after a protracted battle, the new Kansas Legislature overrode Brownback's vetoes, voting to repeal his tax cuts and enact tax increases.[cite: 10]

Brownback, who had a 66% disapproval rating after the repeal of his signature law, left office in 2018 as one of the least popular governors in the country.[cite: 10]

The Kansas City Star was named a finalist in the Public Service category for a 2018 Pulitzer Prize due to its series entitled "Why so secret, Kansas?" The Star reported that Kansas's already-secretive state government had only grown worse under Brownback.[cite: 10]

Source: Wikipedia contributors. (n.d.). Sam Brownback: Tenure. Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia. Retrieved from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Brownback.[cite: 10]
